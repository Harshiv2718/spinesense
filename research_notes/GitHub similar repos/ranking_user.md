# Ranking from the end-user perspective

Lens: a person with back pain using a free home app with a webcam and optional back sensors. Projects are judged on how close they come to delivering that experience well (coaching quality, safety, usability, real-world readiness) and on whether they are well built. All facts come from `candidates_initial.md`, `candidates_extra.md` and the SpineSense README. Items that are my inference, not stated in those files, are marked as such.

Context: `candidates_extra.md` says no mature open-source rehab coaching app turned up. No candidate delivers the full experience, so the ranking favours the closest partial matches, with weight on coaching, safety and trust. Pure infrastructure libraries rank lower because the user never feels them.

## Honourable mentions (3)

- **xioTechnologies/Fusion** (1,756 stars, MIT): proven, company-maintained Madgwick-based AHRS with CI and a Python package; the best way to check or replace the firmware fusion so the optional back sensors give stable angles, but the user never sees it directly.
- **damihr/knee-form-check** (0 stars, licence not stated): free in-browser webcam squat and lunge checker with nearly the same architecture as SpineSense (MediaPipe, frame gate, out-of-distribution guard, ONNX Runtime Web model, calibration, leave-one-subject-out results), but it is 2026-10-15 new, covers squats and lunges only, measured on 8-9 people, and has no licence, so take ideas, not code (confidence: low-medium on quality).
- **davidpagnon/Sports2D** (325 stars, BSD-3-Clause): single-camera 2D joint angles from a webcam, the closest markerless-kinematics tool to what the SpineSense camera path measures; the files give only a one-line description, so its validation quality is unknown (confidence: low).

## The gap

None of these projects combine a zero-install, on-device browser coach with a back-pain-specific safety system. The candidates show rep counting and form rules (nitro-pose, knee-form-check), posture nudges (open-posture), exercise quality scoring (STGCN-rehab), and protective-behaviour research (EmoPain), but not one joins them into a single product. As far as the candidate files show, none of them has an unoverridable emergency-sign lockout for possible cauda equina signs, clinician clearances that are dated and expire after 30 days, a pain-rating-driven daily plan (rest day at 8/10 or more), a check-in on new or spreading leg symptoms that the camera cannot see, or progression rules that need two comfortable sessions plus a next-day "back to usual" answer. None fuses face, movement and voice discomfort signals against a personal baseline into a response ladder (ease off, pause and ask for a 0-10 rating, then easier level or stop). None ships optional cheap dual-IMU back sensors over Web Bluetooth that give a true lower-back angle, tells hip hinging from lumbar rounding, and saves landmark-plus-sensor clips (no video) as labels for camera-only models. The sensor and validation repos (Fusion, mobgap, ssong47's two-IMU relative angle, the NickSakito777 five-IMU project) cover parts of the sensing but not the coaching, and the pain repos have no app at all. Confidence: medium. This rests on the one-line descriptions and maturity notes in the candidate files, not on reading each repository's source, so a candidate could have a feature its listing does not mention.
