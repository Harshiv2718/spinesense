# Datasets and Methods for Detecting Pain and Pain-Related Protective Behaviour from Video (Face + Body) for SpineSense

Scope: publicly available (on request or open) datasets, realistic accuracy, MediaPipe compatibility, confounders, and ethics/licensing for SpineSense (browser MediaPipe Face Landmarker 478 landmarks + 52 ARKit-style blendshapes; MediaPipe Pose 33 landmarks; one RGB webcam; rule-based PSPI-like grimace score vs calibrated neutral + movement signals).

URLs were checked on 2026-10-16. "Verified (fetched)" means the page content was read. "Verified (indexed only)" means the URL showed up in search results with a matching title, but the server blocked or refused automated fetching, so the terms on that page could not be read. Where no licence text could be found, the entry says "licence not stated".

## Q1. Facial pain datasets: access, licence, size, labels, conditions, MediaPipe convertibility

### Takeaway
Every major spontaneous facial-pain dataset (UNBC-McMaster, BioVid, MIntPAIN, X-ITE) is restricted to academic, non-commercial research. Most of them have to be requested by a faculty member or a permanently employed academic. MIntPAIN's EULA explicitly bans "training or testing of commercial systems", and UNBC bans uploading data to "code or model repositories". All four provide raw RGB video or frames, so MediaPipe blendshapes can be re-extracted. In practice the SpineSense owner can use them only through an academic collaborator, and only for research and validation, not for training a shipped model. The Delaware Pain Database (posed photos, demographically diverse, hosted on OSF) is the only one that looks openly downloadable, but its licence could not be verified.

### Inferences
- UNBC's 320×240 frames are probably too small for MediaPipe Face Landmarker blendshapes to stay stable. Re-extracting blendshapes from UNBC is possible, but noise should be expected. This is untested.
- BioVid, MIntPAIN, X-ITE and AI4Pain-2024 use stimulus-level labels (heat/electrical intensity). These are not observer-coded PSPI, so they measure "was a stimulus applied", not "did the face show pain". UNBC is the only one with frame-level PSPI that matches SpineSense's grimace score.
- None of these datasets are recorded during exercise apart from UNBC (shoulder range of motion), and none are recorded on consumer webcams in homes.
- For SpineSense the most usable facial resource is probably Delaware (if the OSF licence allows) for sanity-checking blendshape responses to pain-like faces across skin tones. It is posed, though, and posed faces differ from spontaneous ones.

- The full BioVid agreement PDF and the X-ITE access agreement were not read. The text of the X-ITE licence is unknown ("licence not stated").

- AI4Pain data licence and stimulus type: not stated on the pages that were fetched.
- No verified video resolution or frame rate for BioVid, MIntPAIN or X-ITE.

## Q2. Body / protective-behaviour datasets (EmoPain, challenges, low back pain exercise datasets)

### Takeaway
EmoPain (UCL/Imperial) is the only dataset that labels protective behaviour (guarding/stiffness, hesitation, bracing/support, abrupt action, limping, rubbing/stimulating) in people with chronic low back pain during exercise. The challenge releases (EmoPain 2020 at FG, AffectMove 2021 at ACII) shared only features (IMU joint angles, sEMG envelopes, OpenFace face features), "due to data protection and ethical constraints". Raw video exists only for participants who consented to video release. Keraal (12 low back pain patients, RGB + BlazePose, BSD-2-Clause listed on the GitHub repo) and KIMORE (Kinect, clinical scores) are open movement-quality datasets. Neither has pain or protective-behaviour labels.

#### Per-dataset notes with sources
- **EmoPain original.** 22 CLBP patients (7 M / 15 F, mean age 50.5) and 28 healthy controls (14 M / 14 F, mean age 37.1). Eight cameras at 1,024×1,024 and 58 fps, including a floor camera that captures the face during forward flexion. Animazoo IGS-190 suit with 18 IMUs at 60 Hz. Four sEMG probes at 1 kHz (upper trapezius and lumbar paraspinals). Bracing/support = a limb supports abnormal weight distribution. Abrupt action = sudden movement unrelated to the intended motion. Limping. Rubbing/stimulating = massaging or touching the affected part, or shaking a hand or leg — [PMC6430129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430129/)
- EmoPain access statement: "The dataset will be made available to the research community via a web-accessible interface" (www.emo-pain.ac.uk). - UCL page (search snippet): protective-behaviour annotations came from 4 domain-expert raters (2 physiotherapists, 2 clinical psychologists). - **EmoPain 2020 Challenge.** Three tasks: (i) pain estimation from facial expressions, (ii) pain recognition from multimodal movement, (iii) protective movement behaviour detection. ### Inferences
- EmoPain's six behaviour categories line up closely with SpineSense's movement signals. Hesitation corresponds to slowing and abandoned reps, guarding/stiffness to range drop and rigid movement, bracing/support to shoulder bracing and broken holds, and rubbing/stimulating (including shaking a limb) to shaking. That makes EmoPain the right taxonomy for labelling SpineSense's own data, even if the raw data cannot be used.
- EmoPain's body data is IMU-based (joint angles), not video keypoints. Converting it to MediaPipe Pose would need the raw multi-view video, which is available only for consenting participants and would need a fresh access request. The challenge features are not MediaPipe-compatible.
- Keraal is the most directly usable movement dataset for a browser pose pipeline: it already ships BlazePose 3D keypoints, LBP patients and error labels. Error labels are not pain labels, though, so it can only help with movement-quality and rep-segmentation logic.

### Gaps
- The terms on the EmoPain, EmoPain@Home and AffectMove pages could not be read (UCL returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch). It is unknown whether EmoPain data are currently released, or under what EULA ("licence not stated").
- AffectMove 2021 participant numbers, baseline and winning F1 were not retrieved (PDF blocked).
- The KIMORE licence and current availability could not be confirmed (server unreachable on 2026-10-16).
- No dataset was found that labels guarding or hesitation from consumer RGB webcam video during home exercise.

## Q3. FACS AU ↔ ARKit/MediaPipe blendshape mapping; papers estimating pain/AUs from MediaPipe

### Takeaway
A public, expert-consensus mapping between all 52 MediaPipe blendshapes and FACS AUs now exists: Turrisi et al., Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 2026 (pre-proof). Ten clinical psychologists reached 88% unanimous and 98% majority agreement. They mapped eyeSquint to AU7 and eyeBlink L/R jointly to AU43 or AU45 (depending on how long the eyes stay closed). The authors state the mapping has not been validated against real FACS-coded video. No peer-reviewed paper was found that estimates PSPI from MediaPipe blendshapes and validates it on UNBC.

### Cited Findings
- Turrisi, Iacono Isidoro, … Pioggia, Tartarisco (CNR Italy), "Blendshape features meet action units: a clinical mapping for enhancing facial expression analysis", Computers in Human Behavior Reports, DOI 10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101125, accepted 20 May 2026 (journal pre-proof) — [CNR IRIS pre-proof PDF](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- The mapping links "each of the 52 MediaPipe (version 0.10.35) facial blendshape coefficients" to AUs, built by "ten licensed clinical psychologists and psychotherapists". "88% of mappings reached unanimous agreement… 98% were supported by a majority (i.e., 6/10)". Fleiss' κ was 0.799 in Round 1 and 0.919 in Round 2. Complete tables are in Supplementary Table S1 — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- PSPI-relevant mappings stated in the text: "all experts identified AU7 (left/right) as corresponding to eye squint left and eye squint right", with 6/10 also suggesting AU44. "Eye blink left and eye blink right, corresponding to L45 and R45, can codify both AU43 and AU45 when combined, depending on the duration the eyes are kept closed". The primary correspondences for "jaw, nose, brow, eye and cheek movements achieved 100% agreement". Brow and cheek regions reached κ = 1.00 in Round 2. Cheek squint L/R plus mouth smile L/R together code happiness — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- The authors' own limitation: the mapping was "established through expert consensus on semantic and visual definitions, without empirical validation against real facial behavior data". Future work should validate against FACS-coded video (BP4D, DISFA, Aff-Wild2) and EMG — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- MediaPipe blendshape accuracy as reported in the same paper: on 511 multi-view samples with ground-truth 3D meshes, mean absolute deviation was 0.199 (±0.237). The error gap across gender was ~0.004 and across skin-tone groups ~0.008. Performance degrades with >50% face occlusion, head rotation beyond ~80°, poor lighting, or motion blur — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- The same paper says automatic AU detectors achieve "moderate accuracy (around 50-60%)" (citing Gavrilescu & Vizireanu 2019) — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- PSPI definition: AU4 + max(AU6, AU7) + max(AU9, AU10) + AU43. AUs are scored 0–5 and AU43 is binary, giving a 0–16 range — [Green et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11132v1) (PSPI 0–16); AU list per [search summary of MDPI J. Imaging review report](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/11/5/151/review_report)
- Engineering example: the PyLips robot-face tutorial defines a hand-set blendshape-to-AU weight table (e.g., 'jawOpen': ['AU26', 5]) with symmetry and smoothing steps — [PyLips docs](https://pylips.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/teleoperate.html) (via search snippet)
- The FACSvatar project discussion argues that AU combinations do not influence landmarks linearly, and suggests ML trained on AU-labelled data instead of hand-set weights — [FACSvatar GitHub issue #33](https://github.com/NumesSanguis/FACSvatar/issues/33) (via search snippet)
- A peer-review report for an MDPI J. Imaging article describes using MediaPipe Face Mesh (v0.10.3, 468 landmarks) to compute AUs for PSPI scoring. This is a review-report excerpt, not a validated result — [MDPI J. Imaging 11(5):151 review report](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/11/5/151/review_report)
- Landmark-only pain estimation on UNBC (not MediaPipe) is competitive. Szczapa et al. model landmark dynamics for self-reported pain and report "competitive results… while only considering the shape of the face" — [arXiv 2006.13882 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.13882). An unpersonalised landmark HCRF had VAS MAE 2.80 — [Martinez et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.07154)

### Inferences
- SpineSense's current pairings (browDown to AU4, eyeSquint to AU7, cheekSquint to AU6, noseSneer to AU9, mouthUpperUp to AU10, eyeBlink sustained to AU43) agree with the published expert mapping where its text is explicit: AU7, AU43/45, and cheek squint as part of the AU6/smile cluster. The exact browDown→AU4, noseSneer→AU9 and mouthUpperUp→AU10 entries are in the paper's Figure 4 and Table S1, which could not be read as text. They follow ARKit naming but were not verified here.
- The AU43 vs AU45 distinction (eye closure vs blink) depends on duration, so SpineSense should require sustained eyeBlink (e.g., more than a typical blink length) before counting AU43. Otherwise ordinary blinks will inflate the score.
- Because blendshapes are not calibrated AU intensities (0–5), a "PSPI-like" score built from them is a relative index. It should be compared against each user's own neutral baseline, as SpineSense already does, and not against absolute PSPI thresholds from the literature.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that validates MediaPipe blendshapes against FACS-coded pain AUs (UNBC) or reports PSPI MAE/PCC from MediaPipe features.
- The full Table S1 (supplementary) of Turrisi et al. was not accessed.

## Q4. State-of-the-art accuracy and generalisation (new people, exercise conditions)

### Takeaway
Under subject-independent (leave-one-subject-out) protocols, the best frame-level PSPI regression on UNBC reaches PCC ≈ 0.84 and MSE ≈ 0.53 (2018). BioVid "no pain vs strongest heat" from video alone sits around 71–79% accuracy (chance 50%). AI4Pain 3-class video reaches ~40–54% (chance 33%). During exercise (EmoPain), facial pain estimation is barely above chance (baseline test CCC 0.06). Protective-behaviour detection from IMU+sEMG reaches mean F1 ≈ 0.82 in LOSO research models, against a baseline protective-class F1 of 0.25. Very high accuracies (>95%) appear only when the same people are in both train and test sets. Lab-to-clinic transfer drops sharply (PCC 0.63 on UNBC vs 0.22 in an emergency department).

### Cited Findings
- **UNBC PSPI regression, LOSO, 25 subjects** (comparison table): Kaltwang et al. MSE 1.39 / PCC 0.59 / ICC 0.50; Florea et al. MSE 1.21 / PCC 0.53; Zhao et al. PCC 0.60 / ICC 0.56; Zhou et al. MSE 1.54 / PCC 0.64; Rodriguez et al. MSE 0.74 / PCC 0.78 / ICC 0.45; Tran et al. (C3D) MSE 0.71 / PCC 0.81 / ICC 0.41; Tavakolian & Hadid (proposed) MSE 0.53 / PCC 0.84 / ICC 0.75 — [Tavakolian & Hadid 2018, arXiv 1806.06793](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.06793v1)
- **Within-subject vs cross-subject gap (UNBC):** Hammal & Cohn reported per-level pain intensity F1 of 91–96% with 5-fold CV but only 40–67% with leave-one-subject-out CV, and ICC 0.85 vs 0.55 — [search snippet, from review arXiv 1811.07988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.07988)
- **Leaky protocols inflate results:** Green et al. 2025 used per-participant stratified 60/20/20 splits (so the same people appear across splits) and report binary pain accuracy of 95.1–98.1% and AUC 0.92–0.997 (ViT 0.9806 accuracy) — [arXiv 2506.11132](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11132v1). Benavent-Lledo et al. 2023 report >96% single-frame accuracy on UNBC — [search snippet, Sensors 2023](https://rua.ua.es/dspace/bitstream/10045/139249/1/Benavent-Lledo_etal_2023_Sensors.pdf) (protocol not verified)
- **UNBC self-reported VAS (sequence-level, subject-independent 15/10 split):** best personalised MAE 2.46 and ICC 0.34–0.36 on a 0–10 scale; without personalisation MAE 3.67 and ICC 0.04 — [Martinez et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.07154)
- **UNBC video-level and clinical transfer:** EDi Pain (2025) reports UNBC MAE 1.85, PCC 0.63; on 931 prospective emergency department patients (NTUH), MAE 1.48 but PCC 0.22 — [J. Imaging Informatics in Medicine 2025, NTU record](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/731558)
- **BioVid Part A, NP vs P4, LOSO on 87 subjects:** PainFormer RGB video 76.29% accuracy (F1 75.56); RGB+thermal+depth fusion 76.55%; GSR alone 88.99%; video+GSR 89.08%. Prior video results cited: Vision-MLP 71.03, 2D CNN 71.30, Transformer 73.28 and 77.10, STAGCN 77.30, 3D CNN 77.50, 3D CNN with rPPG 78.90, 3D CNN with pseudo heart rate 88.10 — [PainFormer, arXiv 2505.01571](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01571)
- **AI4Pain 2024 (3-class):** official baseline facial video 40.0% validation / 40.1% test — [Canberra record](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/publications/the-ai4pain-grand-challenge-2024-advancing-pain-assessment-with-m/). PainFormer RGB video 53.67% test, video+fNIRS 55.69% (claimed best) — [arXiv 2505.01571](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01571)
- **EmoPain 2020, face pain during exercise:** best baseline (decision fusion) test MAE 0.91, RMSE 1.41, PCC 0.10, CCC 0.06; validation CCC 0.18. "the recognition of pain intensities from the face is still challenging" — [arXiv 2001.07739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07739)
- **EmoPain 2020, body pain 3-class from MoCap+sEMG:** best LOSO SVM F1 0.41 / MCC 0.19 / accuracy 0.44; hold-out kNN average F1 0.31, MCC 0.02, accuracy 0.35 ("only about as good as chance-level") — [arXiv 2001.07739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07739)
- **EmoPain 2020, protective behaviour (stacked-LSTM baseline):** validation predicted everything as non-protective (average F1 0.4811). Test non-protective F1 0.9029, protective F1 0.2465, average F1 0.5747, accuracy 0.828 — [arXiv 2001.07739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07739)
- **Protective behaviour research SOTA (IMU+sEMG, LOSO):** mean F1 0.82; per activity 0.77 bend-down, 0.81 one-leg-stand, 0.72 sit-to-stand, 0.83 stand-to-sit, 0.67 reach-forward — [Wang et al., "Chronic-Pain Protective Behavior Detection with Deep Learning", arXiv 1902.08990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08990)
- **EmoPain original face baseline:** face pain detection AUC 0.658, F1 0.446 (best feature set) — [PMC6430129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430129/)
- The challenge paper notes that most facial pain work assumes "constrained, ideal settings" with "high resolution, near frontal and unobstructed faces", whereas EmoPain involves movement — [arXiv 2001.07739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07739)
- MediaPipe blendshapes degrade with head rotation beyond ~80°, more than 50% occlusion, poor light, and motion blur — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)

### Inferences
- A realistic target for a cross-person facial pain detector in an exercise app is modest: correlation around 0.1–0.6 with observer or self-report labels, and binary accuracy around 70–78% for clear "strong pain vs none" contrasts. Lab results (PCC 0.84) are an upper bound reached with frontal, still faces and FACS-coded labels.
- Exercise conditions (EmoPain: CCC 0.06 test) are the closest analogue to SpineSense. They suggest the face channel alone is weak during movement, so fusing face and movement signals is justified.
- Body-based protective-behaviour detection (F1 ~0.8 in LOSO research) is the stronger channel, but those results use body-worn IMUs and back-muscle EMG, not webcam pose. Webcam-only performance is unknown.
- No source was found on how sweat specifically affects facial pain detection.

### Gaps
- No webcam/MediaPipe-based benchmark of protective-behaviour detection was found.
- AffectMove 2021 winning F1 was not retrieved.
- More recent (2023–2026) UNBC PSPI LOSO SOTA tables were not retrieved. The 2018 table is the most recent verified comparison here. The hyper.ai leaderboard (PCC 0.71 entry) could not be fetched (HTTP 429).
- No study measured the effect of sweat or exercise-induced flushing on facial pain detection.

## Q5. Confounders: effort vs pain faces, individual differences, demographic bias, personal calibration

### Takeaway
Effort and pain share the frown (corrugator / AU4), and the face of effort also includes jaw clenching. Brow lowering alone therefore cannot separate pain from effort, which is a real risk for an exercise app. Facial expressiveness varies a lot between people. Self-report correlates weakly with facial expression, and personalisation using a few of a person's own sequences cut VAS MAE from 3.67 to ~2.46 on UNBC. Published audits find gender bias in UNBC-trained models and age/ethnicity performance gaps on synthetic data. UNBC and BioVid lack older adults.

### Cited Findings
- **Effort face:** de Morree & Marcora (2010) found that facial EMG (frowning muscle), ratings of perceived effort and leg EMG all rose with task difficulty and muscle fatigue, and correlated with each other — [Kent repository record (via search snippet)](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/26250)
- Jaw clenching may be an important cue for reporting perceived effort during high-intensity exercise — [J. Sports Sci. Med. 2014 13(4):921 (via search snippet)](https://jssm.org/13-4-921.p_d_f)
- **Pain-face cues used by observers:** a Bubbles-method study found that accurate pain discrimination relies mostly on frown lines and the mouth — [European Journal of Pain 2015, doi 10.1002/ejp.676 (via search snippet)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/ejp.676)
- No study was found that directly tests automatic discrimination of pain faces vs exertion faces (search returned none).
- **Individual differences:** "many studies have found low correlations between facial expression and self-reported pain" (citing Prkachin & Solomon 2008). Men and women show different levels of pain expression for the same stimulus. Per-person VAS-vs-OPI patterns can be linear, uncorrelated, or bimodal — [Martinez et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.07154)
- **Personal calibration evidence:** using 1–2 of the test person's own sequences to compute an individual facial expressiveness score improved the RNN-HCRF from MAE 3.67 / ICC 0.04 to MAE 2.47 / ICC 0.36. It did not help every subject (subjects 5, 21 and 13 did not improve) — [Martinez et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.07154)
- **Rater disagreement:** EmoPain raw inter-rater ICC for face pain was 0.102, and binary pain/no-pain agreement κ/α 0.393 (fair). Agreement on body behaviour was low (e.g., α = 0.18 for one bracing subset), and face–body label concordance ranged from α −0.01 to 0.13 — [PMC6430129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430129/)
- **Gender bias:** all four models (linear SVM, RBF SVM, CNN, ViT) trained on UNBC "exhibited gender-based biases". Equal-opportunity gap ranged from 0.0268 (ViT) to 0.0908 (RBF SVM). Existing datasets including UNBC "suffer from insufficient demographic representation" — [Green et al., FG 2025, arXiv 2506.11132](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11132v1)
- **Age/ethnicity bias (synthetic test):** FaceReader AU detection failed on 11.4% of Black faces vs 1.0% of White faces, and 6.84% of older vs 1.16% of younger faces. A pretrained pain model scored AUROC 0.729 on young vs 0.663 on old faces, with South Asian lowest (0.667) and older East Asian faces at 0.623. UNBC-McMaster and BioVid lack older adults — [SynPain, arXiv 2507.19673](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19673v3)
- **Skin tone in MediaPipe itself:** the blendshape error gap across skin-tone groups was about 0.008 MAD (as reported in Turrisi et al. 2026, citing the MediaPipe evaluation) — [Turrisi et al. 2026](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/b57d9fa2-3eb6-403b-8190-dbe4bbda0a16/Blendshape%20features%20meet%20action%20units%20pre-proof.pdf)
- **Demographics of DPD:** White 111, Black 53, Asian 33, Latinx 27, Other 5; ages 18–34 only — [PMC7587421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7587421/)
- EmoPain CLBP participants had a mean age of 50.5, closer to a back-pain app's users than lab cohorts — [PMC6430129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430129/)

### Inferences
- SpineSense's grimace score is at highest risk of false positives during hard concentric reps and long holds, where effort brow-lowering and jaw clenching are expected. Weighting orbital tightening (AU6/7), levator contraction (AU9/10) and sustained eye closure (AU43) above AU4, and requiring agreement with movement signals, would reduce false positives. This is an inference that has not been tested.
- Calibrating against each user's own neutral face, which SpineSense already does, is supported by the personalisation literature. Adding an "effort baseline" (the user's face during a known easy-but-effortful rep) could help separate effort from pain. No source tests this.
- Since labelled pain datasets skew young and White (UNBC, BioVid) or are posed (DPD), any learned model should be checked on older and darker-skinned users before claims are made.

### Gaps
- No published skin-tone audit of pain detection on UNBC or BioVid was found.
- No dataset pairs effort/exertion faces with pain faces in the same people.
- The full texts of the effort-face studies (de Morree & Marcora; JSSM 2014; EJP 2015) were not read. The claims come from search snippets.

## Q6. Ethics, data protection, licensing for deploying a free public app

### Takeaway
Video of faces processed to infer pain is "data concerning health" under GDPR Art. 9, and possibly biometric data. Processing it requires an Art. 9(2) condition such as explicit consent for specified purposes. Under the EU AI Act, recital 18 says physical states "such as pain or fatigue" are not emotions, so a pain/discomfort detector appears to fall outside the "emotion recognition system" definition. On licensing, UNBC, BioVid and MIntPAIN restrict use to non-commercial academic research, and MIntPAIN explicitly bans training or testing commercial systems and any non-academic use. Shipping a model trained on them in a public app, even a free one, is not clearly allowed and would need written permission from the dataset owners.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 9(1) prohibits processing of "genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, data concerning health" (among other categories). Art. 9(2)(a) lifts the prohibition where "the data subject has given explicit consent to the processing of those personal data for one or more specified purposes" — [GDPR Art. 9, gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- EU AI Act recital 18: physical states such as pain or fatigue are not included in the definition of emotion recognition system. Systems detecting fatigue in pilots/drivers to prevent accidents are given as excluded examples. Detecting readily apparent expressions is not covered unless used to infer emotions — [AI Act recital 18 (via search summary)](https://www.artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/18/); commentary [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/what-is-an-emotion-recognition-system-under-the-eus-artificial-intelligence-act-part-1)
- UNBC: "non-commercial research and teaching only"; no redistribution; no uploading to "code or model repositories"; faculty-only requests — [UNBC request page](https://painarchive.jeffcohn.net/request)
- BioVid: "available for non-commercial research only"; signed by permanent academic staff — [BioVid page](https://www.nit.ovgu.de/BioVid.html)
- MIntPAIN: no "nonacademic purpose", including "training or testing of commercial systems" and "developing governmental systems used in public spaces"; academic research only — [MIntPAIN EULA](https://www.vap.aau.dk/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/MIntPAIN_EULA.pdf)
- EmoPain: challenge organisers withheld video "due to data protection and ethical constraints" — [arXiv 2001.07739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07739). Video is shared only for participants who consented to video release — [PMC6430129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430129/)
- Delaware: of the models, 24 consented to experimental use only and are not posted. Models "could opt out entirely or opt out of specific usages" — [PMC7587421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7587421/)
- Keraal: participants "signed informed consent forms"; videos described as anonymised; BSD-2-Clause listed on the repo — [GitHub KeraalDataset](https://github.com/nguyensmai/KeraalDataset)

### Inferences
- "Free" is not the same as "non-commercial academic research". A public consumer app is a non-academic deployment, so under the MIntPAIN EULA it would be prohibited, and under the UNBC and BioVid terms it is at best unclear. The SpineSense owner, as an individual without a faculty/permanent academic post, cannot sign the UNBC, BioVid or MIntPAIN agreements directly.
- Whether trained model weights count as "portions" or derived data of the dataset is not addressed in any terms that were read. UNBC's ban on uploading data to "model repositories" suggests the owners are sensitive to model release. Written permission is needed before shipping any learned weights.
- The lowest-risk path that the sources support:
  1. Keep the rule-based detector, calibrated to each user, and processed on the device. SpineSense already runs MediaPipe in the browser, which supports data minimisation.
  2. Use restricted datasets only for offline research validation, through an academic partner, without shipping derived weights.
  3. Use openly licensed data (Keraal, BSD-2-Clause per repo) for movement logic, and Delaware for qualitative checks if its OSF licence permits.
  4. Collect the app's own consented data under explicit GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) consent if training a model.
  This is legal-adjacent reasoning, not legal advice.
- If face video never leaves the browser and only aggregate scores are kept, the GDPR footprint shrinks. Inferred pain or discomfort scores linked to a user are still plausibly "data concerning health". The AI Act recital 18 exclusion relies on the system detecting a physical state (pain), not emotions. If the app also inferred frustration or anxiety, it could fall inside the definition.

### Gaps
- No full-text EULA was read for BioVid, X-ITE, EmoPain or AI4Pain ("licence not stated" for X-ITE, EmoPain, AI4Pain, Delaware, KIMORE).
- No guidance was found from dataset owners on releasing models trained on their data.
- No EDPB or ICO guidance specific to on-device facial pain inference was retrieved.
- The text of AI Act recital 18 was read through search summaries, not fetched directly.
