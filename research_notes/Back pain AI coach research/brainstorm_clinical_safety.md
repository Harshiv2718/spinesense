## Top 5 datasets to train with first (ranked)

*Order of use matters more than the ranking. Run the existing angle rules against these sets first, as test sets, and train small models only where a rule loses. Hand-written angle rules beat a learned classifier on 4 of 6 poses in IMCD, and KERAAL's published deep baselines are weak (31-54% on 4-class error). No weights should ship until the licence is cleared in writing. None of the 5 gives a clean, commercially safe way to train a deployable pain or discomfort model.*

1. **KERAAL** | Trains: quality and error scoring (error type, body part, time window) and rep segmentation for flank stretch (side bend), torso rotation and arms-raised; also measures the Kinect-to-BlazePose gap | Why: the only LBP-patient set that already ships MediaPipe-family BlazePose 33 keypoints plus RGB and clinician error labels (DEP KQ1, DPB Q2). Caveats: only about 12 annotated patients, annotator kappa 0.63, and "hiding face" is only a partial match to arm raise | Licence caveat: the repo is listed as BSD-2-Clause, but the data licence is not stated and it is unclear whether BSD covers the data. Get written confirmation from the authors (IMT Atlantique / CHU Brest) that training and shipping derived weights is allowed. The videos are anonymised patient videos, so do not redistribute them.
2. **REHAB24-6 and REHAB26-ViewAngles** | Trains: rep segmentation and counting, correct/incorrect with error type, and camera-angle robustness (0/45/90 degrees, which informs the `front`/`side` view guidance) | Why: the only sets with rep boundaries plus correctness, RGB, and (REHAB26) native MediaPipe 33-joint data; REHAB24-6 adds OptiTrack ground truth to measure MediaPipe error. Caveats: arm abduction, squat and shoulder extension only, healthy people, 10 and 5 subjects | Licence caveat: CC BY-NC 4.0 (academic and non-profit only; commercial use needs the authors). A free public app is not clearly "non-commercial academic", so use it for internal evaluation only and get written permission before shipping any weights.
3. **KIMORE** | Trains: quality-score regression (clinical score 0-50) for arm lifting, lateral trunk tilt (side bend), trunk rotation and squat; includes LBP patients | Why: clinician-scored back-rehab exercises with RGB, so MediaPipe can be re-extracted. Use 2D and angle features, because MediaPipe's weak z-axis hurt pelvis rotation (DEP KQ3) | Licence caveat: no licence stated, and the VRAI server refused connections on 2026-10-16, so availability is unverified. Treat as all rights reserved and ask the lab. Segmented skeleton copies on third-party mirrors lack the RGB you need.
4. **UI-PRMD** | Trains: pretraining and correct-versus-non-optimal scoring for sit-to-stand, squat and shoulder abduction or scaption, plus per-rep segmentation | Why: the only clearly permissive source among the top 5. Caveats: skeleton-only (Vicon and Kinect), 10 healthy adults, needs a Kinect-to-MediaPipe joint mapping, and "incorrect" reps are non-optimal rather than clinically labelled | Licence caveat: ODC PDDL v1.0 (public domain) per the paper, but the official page 404s, so use mirrors (EGCN GitHub, Rehab-Pile) and check integrity against the paper. Add KINECAL (CC0, ages 18-92, 5x sit-to-stand, skeleton only) as a companion for older-adult sit-to-stand priors.
5. **EmoPain (and EmoPain@Home)** | Trains / informs: the discomfort and protective-behaviour logic, mainly as a taxonomy and validation reference (guarding, hesitation, bracing, abrupt action, limping, rubbing) mapped to SpineSense's guard, tempo, shrug and shake signals | Why: the only dataset with chronic-LBP protective-behaviour labels during sit-to-stand and bend-down (body F1 about 0.82 with IMU plus EMG in research models; webcam performance unknown; face pain during exercise CCC 0.06) | Licence caveat: request-only (Prof. Berthouze, UCL), terms not published, academic use presumed, raw video only for consenting participants, and the challenge releases are features rather than video (not MediaPipe-compatible). Expect no right to ship weights. UNBC-McMaster is faculty-only, "non-commercial research and teaching only", and bans uploading to model repositories, so use it only through an academic partner to validate the grimace score. Do not train or ship from it.

*Not first, and why: BioVid, X-ITE and AI4Pain label the stimulus rather than the face. MIntPAIN explicitly bans training or testing commercial systems. Fitness-AQA, QEVD, FLEX and FLAG3D are non-commercial or research-licensed and cover generic gym lifts. SynPain is synthetic. Delaware is posed and its licence is unverified. Generic exercise recognition adds little, since the user picks the exercise from the plan.*

## 3 things SpineSense must NOT do

1. **Claim or imply it detects, measures, diagnoses or treats pain or a condition, or imply safety from the absence of signals.** This covers wording such as "detects pain", "pain triggers", "Calm", "your spine is unstable", "the safest way to bend", and any therapy claim. It also covers an open-ended LLM chat that writes exercise progressions or answers risk-factor questions; chatbots repeat the posture myth 88% of the time and progress programmes too fast (AI section 4). Intended purpose and claims decide device status (FDA January 2026 wellness guidance, EU MDR, UK MHRA), and PainChek's regulated route is the precedent for "detects pain". Keep to "notices signs you may be straining and checks in".
2. **Let a camera inference override the user's own words or the red-flag rules, or push someone through pain on unvalidated signals.** No override of CES-type flags by a tick-box. No silent dose change from the face channel alone. No level-up from the unvalidated form score alone, or without a next-day "back to usual" answer. No requirement for full range before a hold counts. No streak that punishes stopping when it hurts.
3. **Send anything off the device or use data it should not.** No frames, landmarks, blendshape streams, pain scores or logs go to a server, analytics service or ad pixel. No inference of mood or emotion (that moves it toward AI Act emotion recognition; pain itself is excluded under recital 18). No shipped weights, and no uploading of restricted data to code or model repositories, from datasets whose terms forbid it without written permission (UNBC, BioVid, MIntPAIN, REHAB24-6/26, Fitness-AQA).

## One bold idea (an outlier others might not propose)

**A serverless clinician "Programme Card".** Replace the weak `clearedByClinician` checkbox with a small card that a physio (or the user's own clinician) generates in a page of the same static app and that the patient loads by QR code or link. The card is a compact JSON in the URL fragment, so it is never sent to any server. It carries: the allowed exercises, a maximum level per exercise, excluded movements, a pain ceiling, extra stop rules, and a review date. The app applies it only as additional caps on top of its own local safety rules and never loosens them, and the red-flag gate and the CES flags stay non-overridable. A matching one-page "session summary" goes back to the clinician (item 9). One paid physio visit then gives the patient a personalised, bounded plan, which answers the conflicting-advice and cost concerns (#4, #5) and the PHTI finding that human involvement is the variable that matters. It also gives sceptical physios what they said they want: a safety-first companion that is not a replacement. | Confidence 3/10 | Risks: a clinician-set "prescription" feature may change the intended purpose toward treatment, so regulatory review is needed before building it. The card is a convenience, not a security control (anyone can forge one), so the in-app safety floor must hold without it. Clinician uptake is unknown ("very little uptake" was reported for telehealth in PCR Q4). Cards can go stale, so enforce an expiry date and keep identifiers out of the card.
